HISTORY × GEOGRAPHY~2 min

1816: the year summer disappeared

In April 1815, Mount Tambora in present-day Indonesia produced one of the largest volcanic eruptions in recorded history. The immediate destruction was catastrophic — but the stranger part happened later.

Material blasted high into the atmosphere reduced the sunlight reaching Earth. By 1816, parts of Europe and North America experienced unusually cold weather, crop failures and even summer snow.

THE SURPRISING PART A volcano in Southeast Asia helped alter weather thousands of kilometres away.

It also influenced culture. During a famously cold and rainy stay near Lake Geneva, a group of writers spent more time indoors. One of them, Mary Shelley, began the story that became Frankenstein.

WHY IT MATTERS

Geography is connected. A physical event in one place can influence food, migration, economics and culture on another continent.
